# Macro dashboard and institutional board: how to update and what we track

## 1. Scope

This file covers one local board, the two boards behind it and the public static copy (section 7). All paths and commands below are relative to this folder (`dashboard/`, the repository root). They show data and short descriptions. No board carries decision lines or position advice; the owner asks about positions separately each time. No board has alerts, alert lamps or notifications, and none sends anything to a webhook or chat service.

- **The local board:** http://localhost:8770, started with `python3 server/serve.py`. It is the only local address the owner uses. It has four top-level pages, in this order: 機構持倉 (the institutional board, unchanged), 宏觀 (the macro view, with two sub-pages, 宏觀 and 油價), 新聞與連接 (news and connections) and AI 追蹤 (AI tracking); after the 更新全部數據 button a 讀法 tab (how to read the board, `server/guide.py`, user 2026-11-22: what every indicator measures and how to read it, then three checklists whose 現時 column is read from the board data on each page load and put in a column by definition only, metals sold with stocks or on fundamentals, rotation or a broad decline, a bounce confirmed or not; then the tested numbers, the market-state line and the bounce check with today's three checks and the R29 history by count; no advice). Each is described below. A fifth tab, 估值, appears only on the local board and only while the private file `../valuation and research/board_view.json` exists (written by a script in that folder; each refresh reruns it); the public snapshot never has it.
- **Internal sources:** the macro board (`macro/serve.py`, port 8766) and the institutional board (`institutional/serve.py`, port 8768). `server/serve.py` starts either one that is not already running, forwards `/macro/` to port 8766 and `/inst/` to port 8768, and stops the servers it started when it exits. It renders `/news/` and `/ai/` itself (`server/pages.py`) from the local build's data in `macro/site_private/data/`. While it runs, it also fetches the news headlines in a background thread every 30 minutes, separately from the data refresh (layer 1 under "News and connections" in section 2). These ports are not opened directly. Changes to `server/*.py` take effect when `server/serve.py` is restarted; changes to `macro/index.html`, to the data or to the hand-kept files in `evidence/` take effect after `python3 macro/refresh.py --render-only`.
- **Macro board (internal source):** `macro/`, served from `macro/site_private/` (built by `macro/build_site.py`) by `macro/serve.py`. Its own page has three tabs, Rotation (板塊輪動), Breadth (市寬: the share of S&P 500 members above the 50-day average and the advance-decline line) and Oil (油價), under a header with the 更新數據 button and the source dates (資料更新狀態); the 宏觀 view uses these parts and nothing else.
- **Institutional board (internal source):** `institutional/`, served by `institutional/serve.py`, with two pages, 今日 and 本週 (section 5). The local board shows it unchanged as the 機構持倉 view.
- **Macro view (宏觀):** `server/macro_view.py` regroups the macro board's page as it is served and never writes to `macro/`. If a change to `macro/index.html` stops its `PATCHES` (or the oil, rotation and Brent anchors) from matching, the page falls back to the original layout with a red notice and `PATCHES` must be updated. The view takes the institutional board's look (its palette, system font, flat cards, compact tables and bordered buttons) and uses the dark palette when the reader's system asks for dark colours (`prefers-color-scheme`), or when `data-theme="dark"` is set on the page. ## 2. How to update

### Data refresh (when the user asks for a board update)

Run `python3 macro/refresh.py`, press 更新全部數據 on the local board at http://localhost:8770 (which also refreshes the institutional board), or press 更新數據 inside its 宏觀 view. All three use the same runner, which works in this order:

1. It takes a process lock (a second run is told that an update is already running) and finds the last completed NYSE session from the exchange calendar, allowing a short settling buffer after the close.
2. It copies the code, the current data and only the inputs the build needs (`evidence/` and `ai_cycle_watch/`) into a staging folder under `logs/<session>/<UTC stamp>_data_refresh/`.
3. It runs `build_data.py`, retrying once. This downloads prices, the S&P member list, CFTC, FRED and Treasury data, computes every dataset, and then runs the flow models (`flows.py`), Treasury auctions (`auctions.py`), the funding market (`funding_market.py`), the TGA and MOVE block (`macro_watch.py`, which writes `macro_watch.json`), the cross-asset block (`cross_asset.py`, which downloads six years of Yahoo closes for the havens, volatility indices and private-credit proxies and writes `cross_asset.json`; a failure is printed and never fails the build) and the Oil page (`oil_tracking.py`).
4. It validates the batch: every required dataset is present, stock data are dated to the completed session, Brent is valid, the confirmed-week flags agree, the core breadth and model fields are complete, every rate and credit series carries a source date, and no source date or CFTC report moves backwards.
5. Except with `--render-only`, it fetches the news headlines (`news_feed.py`, below; any error or a five-minute timeout leaves the previous `news_feed.json` in place and never fails the run), collects the dated BondTerminal price and yield-to-worst references for the specified CoreWeave and Oracle bonds into `evidence/borrower_credit.json` and runs `ai_cycle_watch/track.py` (its layer prices are not shown on the board).
6. It renders the page with `build_site.py` and only then swaps the new data and page in, keeping the replaced copies in the run folder's `previous/`.

The runner prints its result, which is also saved as `result.json` beside `execution.log` in the run folder; a full run adds `news_feed` (`fetched` or `previous kept`). After a successful run, the runner deletes the older run folders of the same session, so each session keeps only its latest run folder; nothing reads older runs, and the latest folder's `previous/` holds the data and page that the run replaced. A failed run's folder stays until the next successful run of that session. Read `session` and the `groups` list: each source group (stocks and models, rates, Brent, credit, CFTC, Fed and net liquidity, confirmed week) is marked `new_observation` or `same_observation_dates`. Report which groups are new. Use only this runner for normal builds: do not run `build_data.py` or `build_site.py` directly against the live folders, and do not start a second server on port 8766.

### Evening summary (when the user asks for the evening update)

1. Refresh the data as above, unless the board already shows the latest completed session.
2. Run `python3 macro/daily_facts.py`. It prints the day's facts from the same snapshot (breadth, sector moves against the S&P, CTA and volatility-control readings) and downloads nothing.
3. Collect what the board does not hold: earnings and guidance, policy and data releases, auctions, war and political events, and second-hand positioning reports. Append new dated CDS readings to `cds_reference` in `evidence/borrower_credit.json` and new manual readings to `evidence/oil_tracking.json`. Each reading carries its date, value and unit, provider, an HTTPS source and whether it is second hand. Add only new dates, never carry a value forward, and edit these files only while no refresh is running, because a local refresh writes `borrower_credit.json` back. Update the AI tracker as described below.
4. Write `daily_summaries/<session>.md` and its English mirror `daily_summaries/<session>.en.md` in the format in section 3. The `daily_summaries/` folder sits in `../valuation and research/`, outside this repository; create it when saving the first summary if it does not exist. No board reads it: the evening summary is read in `daily_summaries/`, and the 宏觀 view at http://localhost:8770 shows its own 現在要看 from `server/macro_summary.py`.
5. Update `evidence/ai_tracker.json` and, when the user asks, `evidence/news_connect.json` as described below, then run `python3 macro/refresh.py --render-only`, which rebuilds the local page from the saved data and these files without downloading anything.

When the user asks for an oil flow check, update `evidence/oil_tracking.json` in the same way and finish with `--render-only`.

### War events (by hand, on the 油價 page)

The 戰事最新進展 card reads the key `war` in `evidence/oil_tracking.json` for its hand-kept part. `build_site.py` copies the whole file into the local build's `oil.json` as `manual`. The card's automatic part (最新報道) comes from the news feed (layer 1 under "News and connections" below); nothing here is filled from it automatically, and no rung is taken from a headline.

- `about` and `rule` (shown under the card) say what the card is; `note` (optional, shown under the card heading) is a caveat on the sources; `updated` is the date of the last hand update.
- `ladder` lists the rungs, lowest first, each with `n`, `name` and `means` (the observable event that defines it): 1 談判或停火 (talks or a diplomatic meeting by the parties or mediators, or a ceasefire in force), 2 部署或撤離 (reinforcements, or an order to withdraw embassy or military staff; no attack), 3 代理或有限度攻擊 (attacks by proxy forces, or a single limited strike by a state), 4 直接攻擊軍事目標 (a state strikes military or nuclear sites on the other's territory), 5 攻擊能源設施或航運 (oil fields, refineries, export terminals, pipelines, tankers or merchant ships attacked), 6 荷姆茲海峽關閉或封鎖 (the strait declared closed or blockaded, merchant traffic stopped). The ladder changes only on the user's request.
- `events` is a list, each with `date` (the date of the event, or of the report when the event's own date is not given), `text` (one line), `rung` (the highest rung whose definition the event meets), `provider`, `source` (an HTTPS link that states the event), `second_hand` (true when the source reports someone else's account) and `from` (where in the project it was recorded). Add an event only when its source link states it and the article or document has been opened: a headline alone, a social-media post or forwarded material is not a source, and unconfirmed reports are not added. A party's own claim (for example a statement by one side) is recorded as that party's claim, with the outlet that reported it. The page sorts newest first and drops an event without a date, a link or a known rung.
- The two readings are computed by the page from the list alone: the rung of the most recent event (the higher rung when two share that date), and the highest rung in the list with its latest date and the list's earliest date. No scores, thresholds or time windows other than the list's own range; a higher rung reached earlier is not treated as over. The card prints the days since the latest event, so a stale list shows as stale.
- Edit the file only while no refresh is running, set `updated`, and finish with `python3 macro/refresh.py --render-only`.

### AI tracker (by hand, when the user asks for the evening update)

The AI 追蹤 page (local only) is updated by hand when the user asks for the evening update. It has four parts, in this order.

1. **階段定義** lists the six stages with their defining evidence and marks which one is the frontier and which holds most companies.
2. **現處階段** gives the stage judged from the evidence rows on the page and the date of that judgement. A stage counts only when a row on the page shows its defining evidence. It names the frontier (最前沿, the stage whose evidence has gone furthest, with the companies concerned) and where most companies are (多數公司所在), each supported by short sentences that cite evidence rows, and then what the next stage still lacks. When a stage rests on a single company or a single row, one sentence says so.
3. **之後要看** is one table with the columns 日期, 事件 and 含義（如果……則……）, sorted by date. A date appears only when the company or the Fed has confirmed it in a primary source; board data shows 每次看板刷新 and everything else 未公佈, both at the end. A row whose date has passed is marked 已過，待更新 until the next hand update removes it.
4. **現有證據** lists the readings from latest to earliest in five columns: date, indicator, latest reading, meaning (含義: one short factual sentence on what the reading implies for the stages) and source as a link. A row is added only when its reading has been verified against a primary document (a filing, a company release or call page, a central-bank page, or the index provider's own page), or when the build reads it from board data; board-data rows are labelled 看板數據. Unverified and second-hand items are not recorded, and a row without a dated reading is not shown.

The six stages are our own and follow the funding chain by what can be observed:

- 階段一 自資擴張: capex rises and is paid mainly from operating cash; credit markets are calm.
- 階段二 借錢擴張: capex exceeds operating cash and the gap is filled by bonds, leases and supplier financing; new debt is still placed easily.
- 階段三 貸方質疑: AI borrowers' bond prices fall and their spreads widen; new-issue terms worsen and order books weaken; share prices may not reflect it.
- 階段四 開支放慢: a payer holds or cuts its capex guidance; contracts are delayed or renegotiated; sellers' earnings forecasts are cut.
- 階段五 融資斷裂: refinancing fails, a borrower is cut below investment grade, or provisions or defaults appear.
- 階段六 資產重估: rents fall close to cost, old equipment is impaired, and useful lives are shortened.

The stage is the furthest one whose defining evidence is present. Evidence can reverse, and the reading then moves back. Different companies can be at different stages, which is why the page states both the frontier and where most companies are. No stage uses a numeric threshold, score or probability.

- The data live in `evidence/ai_tracker.json`: `about`; `updated` (the date of the hand update); `reading_rule`; `stages` (`n`, `label`, `name`, `name_en`, `means`); `current` (`judged_on`; `frontier` and `majority`, each with `stage`, `who` and `evidence`; `missing`); `watch` (a list of rows, each with either `date` or `when` set to 未公佈 or 每次看板刷新, `event`, optional `basis` set to 公司確認, 聯儲確認 or 看板數據, and `if`, a list of `if` and `then`); and `indicators` (`group`, `item`, `means`, and either `reading`, `date`, `source` and `url`, or `live` for a board-data row). The `live` keys are `bond:CRWV`, `bond:ORCL`, `hy_oas`, `b_oas` and `sofr_iorb`. `build_site.py` copies the file into the local build, fills each `live` row with the board's latest dated reading and marks it `from_board`, and drops every row without a dated reading.
- At each evening update, set `updated` to today. Add or replace a hand row only after opening the primary document and checking the figure, link that document in `url`, and write its `means`. A figure seen only in a search snippet, a news report, a transcript site or another secondary page is not recorded. Rows taken from filings keep their own dates until a newer filing replaces them. Remove a row when its figure can no longer be confirmed; never carry a value forward or give an old number a new date.
- Update `watch`: remove rows whose date has passed, merge rows for the same event and date, and add a date only when the company or the Fed has confirmed it in a primary source.
- Judge the stage afresh from the rows on the page, set `judged_on` to the date of the update, and rewrite `current`. The page shows only the current judgement.
- Finish with `python3 macro/refresh.py --render-only`.

### News and connections (headlines by script; issues and 含義 by hand when the user asks)

The 新聞與連接 page (local only) has two layers. Layer 1 is fetched by a script at every local refresh and every 30 minutes while the local board runs; layer 2 and every 含義 are written by hand only when the user asks, at the evening update or at the weekly review.

**Layer 1, the headline feed (`macro/news_feed.py`).**

- `refresh.py` runs it in every full run (not `--render-only`). `server/serve.py` also runs it in a background thread while the local board is up: every 30 minutes, and at start when the stored feed (its `attempted_at`) is older than that; it skips a round while `refresh.py` holds its lock (that run fetches the news itself), checks the wall clock once a minute so a computer waking from sleep catches up, never blocks a page load and never raises into the server; a failure is printed to the server's output. `python3 server/serve.py --news-every <minutes>` changes the interval, and `0` switches it off. Each run merges into the stored feed with the same de-duplication. It reads public RSS and Atom feeds only: Google News search feeds for each topic (headline, publisher, time and link) and the official feeds. It stores, per item, only the headline, publisher, published time (UTC), link, topic and whether it is official, never article text, in `macro/data/news_feed.json`.
- The config block at the top of the file is the only place to edit what is fetched: `WINDOW_DAYS` (7: items published in the last seven days are kept), `CAP` (10: per topic, the newest ten reports and the newest ten official items are kept; the page shows the newest ten of each group), `TOPICS` (id, name on the page, and one Google News query, or several), `SEARCH_DAYS` (the search window of a topic when it is not seven days: two for `war`), `PRIORITY` (`war`: a headline that a war search finds is filed under `war`, even when another topic found it first), `SKIP_WORDS` (stock-picking headlines dropped from every feed), `OFFICIAL` (each official feed with its topic, and optional `only` words a title must contain, `skip` words that drop it, and `host`, the site a Google News item must come from) `OFFICIAL_HOSTS` (a Google News item published by one of these sites is also labelled official) and `PUBLISHERS` (the publisher allow-list: site → name shown on the page). The topics, in page order: `ai_memory` AI 晶片及記憶體供應（HBM、DRAM、反壟斷）, `ai_capex` AI 資本開支及融資（超大型雲端、CoreWeave、Oracle、發債）, `fed_liquidity` 聯儲、財政部及流動性（TGA、回購、拍賣）, `credit` 信用市場, `oil_iran` 油市及伊朗戰事（Hormuz）, `china_macro` 中國宏觀, `export_tariffs` 出口管制及關稅 and `war` 戰事最新報道（伊朗、海灣及荷姆茲）, which has three searches over the last two days: Iranian strikes, attacks, missiles or drones with Bahrain, the Gulf, Hormuz, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar or Kuwait; tankers, ports, refineries, terminals or pipelines attacked, struck or exploding in the Gulf, Hormuz, Bahrain or the Red Sea; and Iran with a ceasefire, talks, negotiations or a truce. The 戰事最新進展 card shows this topic's newest ten. The official feeds: Federal Reserve press releases (enforcement actions skipped) and speeches, US Treasury press releases (Treasury publishes no press-release RSS, so a Google News search limited to home.treasury.gov is used, and only items from that site are kept: sanctions skipped under the liquidity topic, and items on Iran, oil or shipping kept under the oil topic), EIA press releases and Today in Energy (oil-related titles only), the Federal Register feed of the Bureau of Industry and Security (information-collection notices skipped) and SEC press releases (credit, debt, bond, Treasury, money-market, private-fund, clearing and fixed-income titles only).
- Sources: official feeds and named established publishers only. A Google News item is kept only when its publisher's site (the host of Google's `<source url>`, or a subdomain of it) is in `OFFICIAL_HOSTS` or `PUBLISHERS`; the page then shows the allow-list name. The allow-list holds wire services, named established news organisations (international, Asian, Middle Eastern, Indian and European), legal and energy or shipping trade newspapers and one exchange. Social media, aggregators of posts or articles (for example Yahoo Finance, Investing.com, TradingView), blogs and newsletters, stock-picking sites, crypto or token sites and unknown sites are not on it, so TokenPost or WccfTech headlines do not pass. Stored items whose publisher is not official and not an allow-list name are dropped at the next merge. The list is in the config block. No social-media account is read.
- Merging: stored items come first, so a stored item keeps its link (hand notes are keyed by link); then items are de-duplicated by link and by near-identical title (the same words once case, punctuation and wire prefixes such as "UPDATE 2-" are ignored), items older than `WINDOW_DAYS` are dropped, and each topic keeps its caps. An item whose link has an entry under `news` in `evidence/news_connect.json` is kept beyond the cap (not beyond the seven days). Items without a title, a date or an http(s) link are left out.
- Failures: a feed that fails (network, HTTP error, timeout, malformed XML) keeps its earlier items and its own `last_ok`, and its error is listed in `feeds` and `errors`; when every feed fails, the previous items stay unchanged and only `attempted_at` and the errors change. `refresh.py` also catches any error or timeout of the whole script. The page shows the time of the last successful fetch and names any failed feed.
- `news_feed.json` is one row: `attempted_at`, `fetched_at` (the last run in which at least one feed answered), `window_days`, `cap`, `topics` (`id`, `name`), `feeds` (`id`, `name`, `topic`, `kind`, `ok`, `n`, `last_ok`, `error`), `errors`, and `items` (`topic`, `title`, `publisher`, `published`, `link`, `official`). Nobody edits it by hand.
- Labels: an item from an official feed or an official site is 官方公告; every other item is 報道 (a headline from a named publisher on the allow-list, not yet opened). A headline is never a fact on the page by itself: a fact in layer 2 is written only after its article or document has been opened, and a headline is never upgraded by being repeated by several sites.

**Layer 2 and the 含義, by hand (`evidence/news_connect.json`).**

- The file holds `about`, `updated` (the date of the hand update), `rule` (the line under the title), `driver` (本週主導因果, one sentence), `issues` and `news`.
- **Sources.** Every fact on the page stands on a source we opened and checked ourselves: a primary document (court docket or order, SEC filing, company release, prepared remarks or call page, government, central-bank or agency data, exchange data, a research firm's own release) or a report by a named established news organisation, each with its name, date and URL. Material the user forwards from other people (X posts, chat posts, newsletters, Substack) only suggests a topic to look at: it is never cited, quoted or paraphrased sentence by sentence and never appears as a source. Social-media posts are never a source. Everything is written in our own words. Something that cannot be checked is simply not on the page; there is no "unverified" category.
- `news` maps a headline's link (exactly as stored in `news_feed.json`) to `note` (the 含義: one short sentence on what the headline means for an issue, written after the article or document has been opened). The page shows it under the headline. Write notes only when the user asks; leave the rest unwritten. A note whose link has left the feed is not shown; remove it at the weekly review.
- Each entry of `issues` has `id`, `title`, `status` (進行中 or 已有結論), `reviewed` (上次檢討日期), `topics` (the feed topics whose headline groups belong to this issue; a new group appears on the page only when an issue or a feed topic is created), `since` (required, orders the cards, never shown) and the three sections of the card: `facts`, `meaning` and `stocks`. **Three sections only, and nothing said twice.** A card shows exactly 事實與連接, 含義 and 受影響股票及影響; there is no `now`, `watch`, `links`, `breaks_if`, `confidence` or `log` in a new card. What those fields held is written inside the three sections: what is going on is the first facts, the checkpoints and the one breaking condition are the last two lines of `meaning`, and a link to another issue is a `{#issue_id}` inside a fact or a `meaning` line. A fact, number or checkpoint is written once, in the card that owns it (the rates move in the rates card, a bond yield in the financing card); every other card refers to that issue instead of restating it, and a test fails when two cards share a 30-character stretch or when the rates move is stated outside the rates card. The old fields are still read when a file has them: `breaks_if` and `watch` are folded into 含義 as the two lines 推翻條件： and 要看：, `now` is shown above the three blocks, and `since`, `confidence`, `links` and `log` are not shown (the links still feed the 議題之間的連接 line). **Numbering and order.** The page numbers the issues 1..N oldest story first, by `since` (when `since` is missing, by the earliest dated fact; a tie is broken by the earliest dated fact, then by file order; an issue with no date goes last), not by the order of the file, so a new issue enters at its place in time and the numbers of the others shift. Because of that, never write an issue number in text: refer to another issue as `{#issue_id}` (for example `見 {#tga_rates_credit}`) in any string of the file, including `news` notes, and the page replaces it with 議題 n at render; a test rejects a typed 議題 followed by a digit. Card numbers, the 議題 n tags on headline groups, the driver chips and the 議題之間的連接 line all use the same order. Write each issue by the 議題解讀指引 below; the depth standard is in that section.
- `driver` (本週主導因果) is either one sentence (old shape) or a list of `{line, issues}` where `issues` are issue ids; with the list shape the lines must together name every issue whose `status` is 進行中 (the page lists any open issue left out, and the test fails). `lever` (optional, one object `{name, issue, text}`) names the one variable several issues share and the issue that holds it; the page shows it at the top of 議題之間的連接.
- `now` (old field, not written in new cards): what is going on is stated by the first one or two facts, so a separate summary would repeat them. An old card that still has it shows it above the three blocks.
- `facts` (事實與連接): one numbered list of about four to six entries, only the facts needed to carry the linkage and support the reading (not every fact found; the headline layer already holds the news), in the order of the chain. Each entry has `text` (the fact and, in the same sentence, where it leads: the next fact, the thesis, or another issue as `{#issue_id}`; related facts from one source are joined into one entry) and `src`, a list of sources, each with `tag`, `name`, `date` and, for the first two tags, `url`. The tags are exactly these four:
  - 已核實（一手文件）: checked against a primary document; `name` names it and `url` links it; `date` is the document's date or period.
  - 報道（具名媒體）: reported by a named established news organisation whose article we opened; `name` is the outlet (and whom it cites, for example a newspaper citing Bloomberg), `date` the report's date, `url` the report.
  - 第三方估算: an estimate or forecast by a named firm in its own release; `name` is the firm, with `url`.
  - 看板數據: a reading from the board's own data or our own tests on it; `name` is the series (or the test file) and `date` its observation date. The value is written in by hand at the review and stays as written until the next review.
  - Correct a fact's wording when the source says something narrower (for example a letter of intent rather than a contract, or a court's reading of allegations rather than a finding of fact).
- `meaning` (含義): three to five short paragraphs, the delve-in, in the order of the 議題解讀指引: the mechanism and which business or valuation line it hits (收入, 利潤率, 成本, 折現率, 融資, 法律), what the board's prices confirm or contradict (the group-level reading; the per-stock numbers are in the table, so they are not written again) and what else moves. It ends with exactly two lines: `推翻條件：` (the single outcome that would end the reading, which becomes the issue's conclusion if it happens) and `要看：` (the dated checkpoints in one line, each with what it would mean and 確認 or 推翻 in brackets; a date only if the company, court or agency confirmed it in its own document, otherwise 未公佈 or 被要求，未確認). The page marks the block once as 推論（依據上面的事實）. Each step rests only on facts on the page (in this card or in the issue it refers to); when the page has no fact on a party, the step says so rather than guessing.
- `stocks` (受影響股票及影響): a tight table, about three to seven rows, merging names that share a channel and direction. Rows have `who` (company or group), `why` (one short line) and, in the old three-column shape, `effect` (the direction of the effect on that business). In the new shape `effect` is a short phrase (受壓, 受惠, 混合, 間接, 不受直接影響) that the page puts in front of `why`; there is no separate 影響（方向） column. Depth fields, optional but expected on every row of a new or rewritten issue: `tickers` (board tickers, as in `macro/data/stocks.json` or `sectors.json`; `BRK-B`, not `BRK.B`), `channel` (which line is hit; one or more of 收入, 利潤率, 成本, 折現率, 融資, 法律, joined by 、; leave it out when none fits), `expected` (what the reading implies for performance relative to the market: 跑贏, 跑輸 or 無關; a direction only, never a price) and `reaction_note` (one hand line, written without numbers, only when something about that row's tape must be said that 含義 has not said). The page adds a 價格反應 column read from the board's own data (one day, one week and one month; the week and month are relative to the S&P 500) and a text label: 符合論點 when the one-week relative return of the row's tickers all point the way `expected` says, 與論點相反 when they all point the other way, 不明 when the tickers disagree, the board has no data, or `expected` is 無關. No ticks or crosses, and no threshold. The column is live board data with its date in the header, not a fact on the issue: it is not copied into `facts` or `reaction_note` and needs no weekly update. Cover both sides: who is hurt, who is helped and who is unaffected. This is business-impact analysis: no price targets, position sizes, buy or sell words or invented numbers. For a macro issue the rows are the sectors or groups sensitive to it, using board data and our tests.
- `watch` (old field, not written in new cards): the dated checkpoints are the 要看： line of `meaning`. A file that still has `watch` is shown folded into 含義 as one 要看： line, dated entries first and a passed date marked 已過，待檢討; the entries' `breaks` and `tickers` (which still count for the watchlist coverage) are not shown.
- `since` (required; the date of the first dated event the reading is about, not the background: for example the day of the first event on the page that the card tries to explain, not the day the card was written; it decides the issue's number, it may not be later than `reviewed`, and it is not shown on the card). `confidence`, `breaks_if` and `log` are old optional fields: not written in new cards and not shown (`breaks_if` is folded into 含義). The reading's history lives in the weekly review and in `rulebook_log.md`, not on the card.
- **Links between issues** are written inline as `{#issue_id}` in a fact or a `meaning` line, where the connection is made (for example 見 `{#tga_rates_credit}`). The page turns each into 議題 n and, from the same references, draws the one line 議題 a → b、c in 議題之間的連接, so there is nothing separate to keep in step. A reference is written when the two issues share a variable (rates, oil, one company) and a move in one changes a business line in the other, and it replaces restating the other issue's fact; it is not written for two issues that merely share a headline day. An old card's `links` are still read for that line.
- No advice, forecasts of prices, position words or numeric cut-offs anywhere on the page. The 價格反應 labels use the sign of a return only.
- **Watchlist coverage (local board only).** `server/serve.py` renders the page with `news_page(local=True)`, which reads the private `../valuation and research/watchlist.md` and `holdings.json`; `export_site.py` calls `news_page()`, which never reads them, so the public site has no coverage line (a test checks this). The line lists the names in the private watch list (and held names or picks in the private holdings file not already in one of them) that no issue's `stocks` or `watch` names by ticker. Which names stand for a group, and the tickers behind each group, are read from the private `../valuation and research/watch_groups.json`; nothing about the list is written in this repository.
- **Weekly review (when the user asks):** for each issue, re-open the sources of the facts the reading turns on and remove a fact that no longer stands, update the 看板數據 facts (re-read every number in `facts` and `meaning` against the board's current data, because those values stay as written until the review), drop checkpoints whose date has passed from 要看： after writing what happened as a fact, rewrite `meaning` and `stocks` from the facts now on the page, set `reviewed`, re-check the 價格反應 labels (a label of 與論點相反 that lasts two reviews means the reading is rewritten or concluded), look for a fact or number now written in two cards and keep it in the card that owns it, look at the 未有議題的主題 strip and the watchlist coverage line and say which gaps are real, and decide whether the issue can be concluded: set `status` to 已有結論 when the reading is confirmed or broken, and say which in the first fact. Remove a concluded issue when the user agrees; the file keeps only current issues. Also remove `news` entries whose links have left the feed (a `news` note that cites a fact number, such as `{#tga_rates_credit} 事實 2`, must be re-checked when facts are reordered), and rewrite `driver`.
- Edit the file only while no refresh is running, and finish with `python3 macro/refresh.py --render-only` (no downloads). `macro/tests/test_news_feed.py` checks the file's shape (the four labels, the issue fields, at most eight facts and three to eight 含義 steps ending with 推翻條件： and 要看：, topics that exist in the feed, no unverified or social-media material, none of the old fields `now`, `watch`, `links`, `breaks_if`, `confidence` and `log` in a card, `since` on every card; `expected` and `channel` values from their lists, every `tickers` item present in the board's stocks or sectors data, no 30-character stretch shared by two cards and the rates move stated only in the rates card, and, when `driver` is a list, every open issue named in it) and the feed's parsing, the publisher allow-list (including that stored items from publishers off the list are dropped and that no social-media source exists), de-duplication (including that fetching the same items twice adds nothing and that items older than seven days are dropped), the war searches and failure handling with saved sample XML in `macro/tests/fixtures/`; `macro/tests/test_war_card.py` checks the war card, the numbered issue cards with their three blocks (on the real file too; an old card's `now` still shows, and its `watch` and `breaks_if` fold into 含義), the labels, the inline-reference line in 議題之間的連接, the driver and strip, and the watchlist coverage, the headline groups (issue topics first, at most ten each) and the background fetch.

### 議題解讀指引 (how every issue on 新聞與連接 is written)

Our own method. It has no numbers or cut-offs. Work through the questions in this order; the answers become the first facts, `meaning` (its last two lines being 推翻條件： and 要看：) and `stocks`. The facts are there only to carry the linkage and support the reading, not to be complete: keep the few that the reading needs and leave the rest to the headline layer.

1. **What is being done, and by whom?** Put the facts side by side and say in plain words what they show together: who is acting, what they are doing and what follows from it. One fact alone is not a reading; the reading is what the facts mean together.
2. **What do the authorities want?** For the government, the regulator, the central bank or the court: what they appear to want, what tools they have, and what comes next by their own procedure (a hearing, a filing deadline, a meeting, a rule). If the page has no fact on them, say so.
3. **What does it do to the businesses?** Does it help or hinder a business model (for example locking up supply with long contracts)? What happens to pricing power, supply access, cost and legal exposure? Who is on the other side and gains?
4. **How would a large institution treat it?** Is it one-off or structural; does it change earnings now or only how long they last? Judge from board data whether it is already priced (relative strength, breadth, rotation, CFTC positioning). Which groups would they re-rate, hedge, reduce or rotate towards, and why? Describe how institutions think, not what to do, and invent no numbers.
5. **What else moves?** Second-order effects on customers, suppliers, competitors and other regions, and any board series that the issue distorts.
6. **What would confirm or break the reading?** Name the next dated events and documents, what each result would mean, and for whom. These become the 要看： line of 含義, each marked 確認 or 推翻; the one outcome that would end the reading becomes the 推翻條件： line.

**Depth standard.** An issue is deep enough when a reader can follow one chain from the news to a line of a named company and then see whether the tape agrees, within three sections and without saying anything twice. Each issue must show:

- **Facts that connect.** Four to six numbered facts in the order of the chain, each saying where it leads. Facts from one source are joined; a fact that another issue owns is referred to as `{#issue_id}`, not repeated.
- **Mechanism.** In 含義, the chain written as event → the line of the business or the valuation it hits → the name (for example 機制（折現率）, 機制（融資成本）). A step that cannot name a line is not yet a mechanism.
- **Channel.** Every stock row says which line is hit (`channel`: 收入, 利潤率, 成本, 折現率, 融資, 法律) and what that implies against the market (`expected`). Two issues on the same company can hit different lines; say which.
- **Price-reaction check.** A step in 含義 states whether the board's own prices agree with the reading (the 價格反應 column and its labels), including the contradictions, and says when the data stops before the event. A reading that the tape contradicts is stated as contradicted; the issue is not tuned to the tape and the tape is not ignored. Say so too when the move has a cause the page cannot show (mark it 未解釋 rather than attribute it).
- **Cross-links.** A `{#issue_id}` wherever the issue shares a variable with another open issue (rates, oil, one company, one financing chain) and a move in one changes a line in the other; an issue that stands alone has none.
- **推翻條件： and 要看：.** The last two lines of 含義: the one outcome that would end the reading, and the dated checkpoints on both sides (確認 and 推翻).
- **Sources.** Unchanged: every fact on a source we opened. Do not copy a number from a search excerpt, a forwarded post or a model's memory; a claim we could only see as an excerpt, or that the outlet itself says it could not verify, is written as what the named party says (「革命衛隊稱…」) with the outlet's tag, or left off.

### Checks before saving a summary

- The summary's session equals the board's session. If another refresh finished while the summary was being written, recheck the data date before rendering.
- Every number carries its own observation date. Weekly and monthly sources (CFTC, the Fed balance sheet, fund-manager surveys, AAII, fund-flow reports) keep their old date until a new release. A carried value, a reloaded page or a later retrieval time is not a new observation, a missing value is a gap and never a zero, and a corrected value is a revision rather than a market move.
- Comparisons are like for like: the previous valid reading of the same series, CCC and BB on matching dates, a rolling five-session return kept apart from the completed week and from week-to-date, and ratio changes in per cent kept apart from return differences in percentage points.
- CFTC figures are Tuesday positions published on Friday. A change in the net share of open interest can come from open interest itself, and a smaller net short is not a new net long.
- CTA and volatility-control figures are model estimates, and price ratios such as HYG ÷ IEF are prices; neither is an observed fund flow, and HYG ÷ IEF is not a credit-spread observation.
- A shrinking drawdown can come from an old high leaving the window, and a basket move is checked for missing members before it is read.
- Second-hand reports are marked 轉述 with their source and date, and a prime broker's client sample describes those clients, not the market.
- Neocloud share prices neither confirm nor deny borrower credit. A gap in borrower quotes limits the AI-funding reading and is not by itself evidence of market-wide stress.

### When a data source fails

- A required download that still fails after the retry, or any failed validation, rejects the whole batch: the board keeps its previous data and `result.json` records the error. Under the runner, a failure in the flow models or `ai_cycle_watch/track.py` also rejects the batch, although the board shows none of the tracker's layer data.
- A FRED outage does not stop the build. The term premium, the ICE credit spreads and the IG yield keep their previous values and dates, net liquidity keeps its previous file, and Treasury yields still update from the Treasury. Treasury auctions, the funding-market data and each Oil block likewise keep their previous file with its own dates; within the five-contract positions block each contract keeps its stored series when its source fails, a contract never fetched is left out and named on the card, and the total runs only to the last week every included contract reports. A failed TGA or MOVE download keeps the previous block in `macro_watch.json` with its own dates, lists the failure in that file's `errors`, and the 宏觀 page says so; `cross_asset.json` does the same for each of its blocks (havens, vol, proxies).
- A news feed that fails keeps its earlier headlines and its own time of last success; the whole news step can fail without affecting the refresh (layer 1 above), and a failed background fetch leaves the stored feed as it was.
- BondTerminal parsing fails closed: a missing or inconsistent quote is skipped, and the previous reference keeps its date.
- Report what failed, which date the board still shows and which summary statements it limits. Do not edit `macro/data/` by hand or relabel a date.

## 3. The summary

The files are `daily_summaries/<session>.md` in Traditional Chinese (書面語) and `<session>.en.md` in English, section for section with nothing extra. An intraday or pre-market edition may be saved under the session's name with a first line `# <title>`; the after-close edition replaces it. The summary contains these parts in this order:

1. `**今日讀數：**` is one line of dated numbers: credit (HYG next to IEF and the latest HY OAS), yields (the 10Y change split into real yield and breakeven), rotation (SMH and the AI baskets, defensives, energy, RSP and IWM, advancers against decliners) and Brent's change on the day. One sentence follows that names the session's main driver in words, as our reading of that evidence.
2. Five or six ranked blocks follow, each headed `## <①–⑥> <block>：<the day's finding>`:
   - **業績**, only on days with relevant results: results and guidance from large, AI-chain or watch-list companies since the previous summary, with their capex and financing news, Asian results as same-session confirmation, and season statistics. On other days, news goes to the block it affects.
   - **債**: the Fed path and its market pricing; 2Y, 10Y and 30Y yields with the 10Y split into real yield, breakeven and term premium; MOVE; Bund, gilt, JGB and OAT; auctions; net liquidity; and whether bonds traded the day as inflation, growth or haven.
   - **信用**: market-wide IG, HY, CCC and BB on matching dates; then AI borrowers (bond references with their caveat, CDS readings, equity proxies); and whether stress passed from equities to credit.
   - **板塊**: rotation or broad selling; leaders and laggards against the S&P, the AI baskets, RSP and IWM against QQQ, defensives and energy, and gold and bitcoin as the cross-asset check.
   - **油價**: Brent and WTI over a day and a week (noting contract rolls), the front against the long end, inventories, gasoline, and whether oil moved breakevens. A war-driven move points to the war block.
   - **戰事**: dated facts with their source quality, and prediction-market odds as market data, with no probabilities of our own.
   - The blocks are ranked afresh each session: first the block with direct evidence for the main move, then blocks whose reading changed direction, then new but unremarkable observations, and last blocks with no new observation, in one line with the old date kept.
3. `## 本週變化` appears only after a completed exchange week. It compares the previous completed-week close with this one, with both dates, and covers broad-market and AI returns, breadth, credit with its source dates, a new CFTC report if one exists (with its report Tuesday and publication date), and at most one rates or model line.
4. `## 結論：市場是否脆弱？<finding>` draws on breadth, credit, volatility, positioning (CFTC, prime-broker and fund-manager surveys, fund flows, AAII, CTA and volatility-control estimates, each with its date) and semiconductor breadth. The market-state reading in the 宏觀 view's 現在要看 (R29) may be quoted as reference, and any base rate is labelled as reference.
5. `## 之後要看（英國時間）` lists numbered, dated checkpoints, and the summary ends there. Caveats, old dates and 轉述 stay inline beside the number they qualify.

Bullets take the form `- **標籤：** 內容`, with at most one indented sub-level. Headings state the day's finding and are not reused from earlier days. The summary contains no holdings, trade instructions, sizing words or position postures.

## 5. Institutional board

- **今日** shows key-level ladders for NQ, ES and VIX (price, moving averages, CTA flip levels, option open-interest walls), the ranges priced by options, the mechanical flows of leveraged ETFs and volatility-control funds for a range of daily moves, and VIX readings (VVIX, VIX against the S&P move, realised against implied volatility). Live prices come from `/api/live` (Yahoo, delayed, reused for about a minute) and are read against levels set at the last close; when the live bar belongs to a later session, the page says the board is stale.
- **本週** shows the four CFTC trader categories for the Nasdaq and the S&P plus the markets with the largest weekly change, the non-dealer E-mini position, the CTA model's largest five-day buys and sells, volatility-control funds on the S&P and the Nasdaq, a volatility reference (asset managers' VIX futures position and realised against implied volatility), and earnings in the next three weeks as Yahoo lists them, unverified.
- `institutional/build.py` writes `cftc.json`, `cta.json`, `vc.json`, `vol.json`, `nondealer.json`, `levels.json`, `level_log.json` (each session's levels, kept for later scoring) and `meta.json` (build time, dates and warnings) into `institutional/data/`. Its sources are CFTC (TFF and disaggregated), Yahoo futures, indices, option chains and ETF assets, and the ECB, Bank of England and Japan Ministry of Finance for the yield markets in the CTA model. Everything except the CFTC numbers and observed prices and open interest is a model estimate; dollar amounts depend on the assumptions in `build.py` (`LOOKBACKS`, `VC_TARGET`, `VC_CAP`, `VC_AUM_BN`, `LEV_ETFS`).
- **Refresh (when the user asks for an institutional-board update):** press 更新數據 on the board or run `python3 institutional/build.py`, which takes about a minute. A module that fails keeps its previous file and is listed in the warnings at the top of the page, and the button saves the build output to `institutional/data/.last_build.log`. CFTC data change once a week; everything else changes every trading day. This folder holds no personal data.
